# The DPTrainer Story

## The Origin

DPTrainer started not as a product idea but as a personal 
problem.

In March 2026, a Trainee DPO on a vessel operating near 
the Pohokura field off New Plymouth, New Zealand, was 
sitting at an aft bridge DP simulator desk trying to make 
the most of his passive training time. The Converteam 
Mermaid Vision system was running. The simulation was 
active. And there was nothing structured to do with it.

So he opened Claude on his phone and asked: give me a 
weather condition and an exercise.

That was the last message of one swing at sea. When he 
came back the following trip, his first message was almost 
identical. Same problem, same solution, same gap.

Somewhere between those two conversations — across two 
separate swings, hundreds of nautical miles, and hours of 
simulator time — the question changed. It stopped being 
"I need exercises to make the most of my DP training" and 
became "TDPOs need exercises to make the most of their 
DP training."

That shift is where DPTrainer was born.

## The Founder

Finn is a New Zealand mariner and Trainee DPO who began 
his career at sea in 2018. A watchkeeper by trade and a 
sailor by instinct, he found his direction in the research 
and oil and gas sector — an industry that demands precision, 
situational awareness, and the kind of ship handling 
capability that takes years to build.

He started his NI Dynamic Positioning qualification because 
the offshore industry represented the best opportunity to 
hone those skills. And because the industry is filled with 
brilliant, experienced people — which makes it competitive, 
which makes preparation matter.

In one sentence: he doesn't want to waste his time. He 
wants to make the most of his training, be as competent as 
he can be as early as he can be, and progress in a career 
that he takes seriously.

He built DPTrainer because he suspected other TDPOs felt 
exactly the same way.

## Why This Matters

The NI requires Trainee DPOs to complete up to 30 passive 
simulator days — a minimum of 60 hours of ship-based 
simulator time. The NI prescribes no specific tasks for 
this time. The expectation is that a supervising DPO 
structures the sessions.

In reality, not every TDPO has a DPO standing next to them 
for two hours. Not every ship has a culture of structured 
simulator training. And without structure, two hours at a 
DP desk is genuinely one of the most passive professional 
experiences in maritime — watching a vessel hold position, 
waiting for something to happen.

Those 60 hours are a serious chance to build real skills. 
The simulator is already an excellent tool. The gap isn't 
the technology — it's the structure around it.

DPTrainer fills that gap. Not by teaching. Not by replacing 
the NI or qualified DPOs. By giving TDPOs a plan, a 
challenge, and a record of what they did with their time.

## The Vision

A TDPO anywhere in the world — on any vessel with a 
ship-based DP simulator — opens DPTrainer at the start of 
their session. They enter their vessel, their environment, 
their training day. The app gives them a task. They execute 
it, record what they observed, answer the debrief questions 
their DPO would have asked. They do it again. Two hours 
later they have a structured, documented session record 
suitable for their NI logbook.

On day one, they're learning to stop a drifting vessel 
cleanly. On day thirty, they're executing compound failure 
scenarios with professional discipline.

Eventually — a web app that works on any tablet today, 
native iOS and Android apps when the product is validated, 
and a partnership with NI-accredited training centres so 
that every student who walks out of an induction course 
walks out with DPTrainer already in their pocket.

It started as one mariner not wanting to waste his time. 
It ends as the structured training companion for every 
TDPO on the water.

## The Build

Built by the founder, assisted by Claude Code, version 
controlled on GitHub. Every major milestone pushed and 
documented. Content validated by certified DPOs. 
Tested in real simulator sessions on a real vessel before 
a single line of marketing is written.

The product exists because the problem exists. The founder 
knows the problem because he lived it, on the 22nd of 
March 2026, at a simulator desk off the coast of New 
Zealand, asking an AI for something to do.
